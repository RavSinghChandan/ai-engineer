Ancient Civilizations: o Egyptians: Ancient Egyptian numerology was based on the belief that numbers were sacred, and they were often used in religious rituals, architecture, and astrology. Pythagoras and Ancient Greece: One of the most significant figures in the development of Western numerology is Pythagoras (c. 570 495 BCE), the Greek philosopher and mathematician. His work formed the foundation of the Pythagorean system of numerology, where numbers 1 through 9 are associated with specific qualities or vibrations. 3. Medieval and Renaissance Europe: Numerology flourished during the Middle Ages and Renaissance, when it was deeply intertwined with astrology, alchemy, and other occult sciences. Scholars like Marsilio Ficino and Giovanni Pico della Mirandola studied the mystical properties of numbers and their connection to divine forces. The Various Systems of Numerology There are different systems of numerology practiced around the world, with the most well-known being: 1. Pythagorean (Western) Numerology: This is the most widely used form of numerology in the Western world, founded by Pythagoras. It is based on the belief that numbers 1 through 9 hold unique energies. The system also includes Master Numbers (11, 22, and 33) and involves calculations based on the full birth name and birth date. 2. Chaldean Numerology: Originating in ancient Babylon, Chaldean numerology assigns different numbers to the letters of the alphabet compared to the Pythagorean system. In this system, the letters are assigned values from 1 to 8 (excluding 9, which is considered sacred). Chaldean numerology focuses more on the vibrations of names, with a belief that certain names or sounds have special significance that can impact a person's life. 3. Kabbalah Numerology: As mentioned, the Kabbalah system originates from Jewish mysticism and is closely tied to the Hebrew alphabet. In Kabbalah, the letters of the alphabet correspond to numbers, and by calculating the number of a name or word, you can interpret its hidden meanings. This system is often used in conjunction with spiritual teachings and is tied to deeper aspects of soul and destiny. 4. Chinese Numerology: In Chinese numerology, the numbers 1 to 9 hold significant cultural and spiritual meanings, often based on phonetic sounds and their association with good or bad luck. For example, 8 is considered an extremely lucky number, symbolizing prosperity and wealth, while 4 is viewed negatively, as it sounds similar to the word for "death" in Chinese. 5. Indian (Vedic) Numerology: Indian numerology is rooted in the Vedic traditions of ancient India. It blends astrological principles with numerological values, where the numbers are associated with planets and celestial bodies. In this system, the birth date, as well as the name, are significant in predicting future events and understanding one s path in life.
