01 · The execution gap

Government knows the beneficiary exists. It does not continuously confirm the outcome happened.

Registration is a solved problem. Every department named in this proposal already runs a system that records who is entitled to what. The gap opens after registration — when a scheduled service is missed and no process notices on the day it lapses.

The registration-to-outcome gap
Where beneficiaries are lost, national figures

Source: NFHS-5 (2019–21), National Family Health Survey, Government of India. The 30-point spread between institutional delivery and completed antenatal care is the execution gap this proposal addresses.

Why the gap persists

  • A missed appointment produces no signal until the monthly report is compiled, by which point the window for intervention has closed.
  • Field staff carry every case at equal weight. Nothing tells an ASHA which three of her ninety households need her today.
  • A dashboard reports what was entered. It cannot report what should have happened and did not.
  • When a case does fail, there is no automatic record of who was responsible and when they were informed.
  • Departments hold adjacent facts about the same household but no shared view of that household.
The distinction that matters

A dashboard answers how many. An execution system answers which ones, whose job, by when, and what happened. This proposal is the second thing.

02 · The operating model

One loop, applied six times

Every mission in this proposal runs the same six-step loop. Only the beneficiary and the service schedule change. This is deliberate: the district learns one model, not six products.

STEP 1
Detect
A due date passes, a threshold is crossed, or a pattern appears across households.
STEP 2
Prioritise
Cases are ranked by risk and consequence, not by date of entry.
STEP 3
Assign
A named field worker receives the case with the context needed to act.
STEP 4
Follow up
If nothing is recorded, the case is raised again without anyone chasing it.
STEP 5
Escalate
On a defined SLA the case moves to the supervising officer.
STEP 6
Verify & close
Closure requires evidence, not a status change.

What sits underneath

The system reads from and writes back to the department's existing records. It does not ask field staff to maintain a parallel register, and it does not become the system of record for anything.

Why this matters for sanction

No department is asked to migrate, replace or decommission a system. The proposal is an orchestration layer, which keeps procurement simple and reversible — if the pilot fails, the district switches it off and loses nothing.

03 · Mission 01

01

Maatri-Jeevan 360

Pregnant woman → mother → child · 1,000 days of care

No mother or child is lost between one care milestone and the next.

1. The situation today

A pregnancy is registered in RCH within the first trimester. From that point the woman is entitled to four antenatal visits, iron and folic acid supplementation, tetanus immunisation, institutional delivery, postnatal visits and a full immunisation schedule for the newborn. Each of these has a due date.

Nationally, 88.6% of births now take place in an institution — a genuine achievement of the last decade. But only 58.1% of women complete the four antenatal visits that make that delivery safe. The system succeeds at the event and fails at the schedule leading up to it.

2. Why the current approach does not close the gap

  • A missed antenatal visit generates no alert. The next contact with the system is often the delivery itself.
  • High-risk pregnancies — anaemia, hypertension, previous complication, age — are recorded but not separated into a working list.
  • An ASHA covers roughly 1,000 population. Nothing tells her which of her cases is closest to harm this week.
  • Iron and folic acid adherence is self-reported at the next visit, so a lapse is discovered months late.
  • After delivery the mother's case effectively closes, and the newborn's immunisation becomes a separate record.

4. What the mission does

  • Builds a dated care calendar the moment a pregnancy is registered, derived from the department's own protocol.
  • Flags a missed antenatal visit on the day it lapses rather than at month end.
  • Ranks each ASHA's working list by clinical risk and days overdue, so the most urgent household is first.
  • Tracks iron and folic acid supply against the 180-day entitlement instead of relying on recall.
  • Prepares a delivery plan — facility, transport, expected date — before the third trimester.
  • Carries the same case forward into postnatal visits and the child's immunisation schedule without re-registration.

6. Division of responsibility

Nothing clinical, financial, legal or disciplinary is decided by software. The system removes the work of noticing, chasing and recording. It does not remove the officer.

Handled by the system

  • Generate the care calendar and keep it current as dates shift
  • Detect a lapsed visit, a missed IFA collection or an overdue immunisation
  • Rank the ASHA and ANM worklists by risk each morning
  • Send the reminder to the household in the local language
  • Escalate to the Medical Officer when the SLA is breached
  • Compile the block summary without anyone preparing a report

Decided by the officer

  • Every clinical judgement, including what counts as high-risk
  • Referral to a higher facility
  • Any change to the treatment or supplementation protocol
  • Release of transport or emergency funds
  • Action against a field worker for non-performance
  • Whether a case is genuinely closed

Harit Rajya 360

Land → sapling → surviving tree · five-year horizon

Plantation is measured by survival, not by saplings counted.

1. The situation today

A plantation target is announced, budgeted and reported as achieved on the day of planting. The photograph is taken, the muster roll is closed, and the sapling becomes somebody's assumption.

Third-party monitoring under the Green India Mission has recorded survival above 70% in assessed plantations, with some categories between 82% and 91%. That is a credible range — but it is measured on a sample, twice a year, after the fact. It is not a live figure the district can act on.

Source: MoEFCC Green India Mission third-party monitoring reports. The final bar is an illustrative comparison for unmaintained sites, not a published national figure — the district should replace it with its own survival audit.

2. Why the current approach does not close the gap

  • Reporting stops on the day of planting. Nothing in the process asks the question again at six months.
  • Species is frequently chosen by nursery availability rather than by soil, rainfall and groundwater at the site.
  • Maintenance has no named owner after the planting contract closes.
  • Survival audits are periodic and sampled, so a failing block is discovered a year late.
  • Contractor performance cannot be compared across sites because outcomes are not recorded per contractor.

4. What the mission does

  • Checks land ownership, soil, rainfall, slope and groundwater against species requirement before sanction.
  • Records a named maintenance owner and a watering schedule as a condition of the plantation record.
  • Triggers a survival check at six months, one year, three years and five years automatically.
  • Separates causes of loss — drought, grazing, fire, wrong species, contractor failure — so the response fits the cause.
  • Reports survival by block, by species and by contractor, making repeat failure visible.
  • Flags sites where survival falls below threshold while replanting is still within season.

Pashu-Jeevan 360

Animal → farmer → veterinary system · disease containment

Disease is detected before it becomes an outbreak and a livelihood loss.

1. The situation today

For a smallholder, one buffalo is a substantial share of household income. When that animal stops eating, the loss is immediate and the household has no buffer.

India runs national programmes against foot-and-mouth disease, brucellosis, PPR and classical swine fever under the Livestock Health and Disease Control Programme, with vaccination campaigns and animal identification. The infrastructure exists. What is missing is early signal: detection currently depends on the farmer deciding, on his own, that this is serious enough to report.

2. Why the current approach does not close the gap

  • Vaccination due dates exist in the record but are not pushed to the farmer or the vaccinator.
  • Symptoms reported in one village are not compared with reports from neighbouring villages.
  • The farmer is the only early-warning sensor, and he reports only when the animal is visibly failing.
  • Outbreak response begins after spread rather than at the first cluster.
  • An animal's history is scattered across visits, so recurring problems are not recognised as recurring.

4. What the mission does

  • Tracks vaccination due dates per animal and routes them to the assigned vaccinator with a route plan.
  • Accepts a simple symptom report from the farmer or field worker without requiring a diagnosis.
  • Compares symptom reports across neighbouring villages to detect a cluster forming.
  • Raises an alert to the block veterinarian when reports cross a department-set threshold.
  • Routes confirmed clusters upward to district and state surveillance automatically.
  • Maintains one continuous health record per animal across visits, vaccinations and treatments.

Shiksha-Jeevan 360

Child → teacher → school → block · learning outcomes

Education is measured by learning recovered, not by enrolment recorded.

1. The situation today

Attendance in a school reads 93% and the school is marked satisfactory. Whether the children in that room can read at grade level is a separate question, and the routine reporting does not ask it.

UDISE+ gives the state genuine scale in education data. Secondary dropout stood at 11.5% nationally in 2024–25, with Bihar reporting 6.9% at that level. Those are useful headline figures — but a district cannot act on a percentage. It can only act on a named child.

Source: UDISE+ / Ministry of Education, Rajya Sabha unstarred question 2133 (2024–25). A single state figure conceals wide block-level variation, which is why intervention has to work at child level rather than district level.

2. Why the current approach does not close the gap

  • Enrolment and attendance are tracked continuously; learning is assessed occasionally and rarely acted on.
  • Dropout is recorded after the child has already stopped attending, when recovery is hardest.
  • A child falling behind is visible to one teacher, and that knowledge does not survive a transfer.
  • Block officers see school-level aggregates, so a school with three failing children looks identical to one with thirty.
  • There is no record of whether an intervention was tried, or whether it worked.

4. What the mission does

  • Combines falling attendance with falling assessment scores, which together predict dropout better than either alone.
  • Produces a named list for each teacher rather than a school-level percentage.
  • Suggests an intervention appropriate to the pattern — remedial support, family contact, or referral.
  • Tracks whether the intervention was delivered and whether the child recovered.
  • Shows the block officer which schools have rising unresolved cases, not just which have low averages.
  • Follows the child across grade transition, when most silent dropout occurs.

Kisan-Jeevan 360

Farmer → land → crop → market · income outcome

Farmer decisions are supported from soil selection through to sale.

1. The situation today

The farmer's problem is not how to grow wheat. It is what to grow, when to sow, how much to spend, what risk he is carrying, and where the crop will be sold.

Bharat-VISTAAR, launched in 2026, gives farmers multilingual advisory, weather-based guidance and market information. That solves the information problem at the individual level. It does not solve the coordination problem: fifty thousand farmers each receiving good individual advice can still collectively plant the same crop and collapse their own price.

2. Why the current approach does not close the gap

  • Advisory ends at sowing, but the income problem is concentrated at selling.
  • No one aggregates what a district is collectively planting until the crop arrives at the mandi.
  • Storage allocation and procurement planning begin after harvest, when the price has already moved.
  • Risk sits entirely with the individual farmer, who has no view of what his neighbours are planting.
  • Scheme eligibility is discovered late, often after the input purchase that would have qualified.

4. What the mission does

  • Matches crop options to the farmer's own soil, water availability and nearest market rather than to a generic district advisory.
  • Presents options with expected cost, expected yield and risk level, leaving the decision with the farmer.
  • Aggregates sowing intent across the district before the season rather than after harvest.
  • Warns the administration when projected output exceeds combined mandi and storage capacity.
  • Gives procurement, storage and FPO aggregation a number to plan against, weeks ahead.
  • Surfaces scheme eligibility at the point the decision is being made, not afterwards.

1. The situation today

One household has a ration problem, a pension problem and a water supply problem. Today that is three departments, three complaint numbers and three separate follow-ups — and the citizen is the only person holding the thread together.

That is backwards. The government should be the integration layer, not the citizen. This mission is the one that makes the other five work together, because it establishes the routing, deadline and escalation discipline that all of them depend on.

2. Why the current approach does not close the gap

  • The citizen is the integration layer between departments that do not talk to each other.
  • A grievance closes on the file when a status is changed, not when the problem stops existing.
  • SLA breaches surface at a review meeting, by which time the deadline is long past.
  • Repeat complaints from the same locality are treated as separate cases rather than one root cause.
  • There is no consistent record of which officer was informed, when, and what they did.

4. What the mission does

  • Routes a complaint to the correct department, office and jurisdiction automatically from its content.

6. Division of responsibility

Nothing clinical, financial, legal or disciplinary is decided by software. The system removes the work of noticing, chasing and recording. It does not remove the officer.

Handled by the system

  • Set the deadline and name the responsible officer
  • Follow up before the deadline rather than after
  • Escalate automatically on breach
  • Group repeat complaints to expose a root cause
  • Produce the department-wise SLA view for review

Decided by the officer

  • Every substantive decision on the merits of the complaint
  • Any decision that denies or withdraws a benefit
  • Sanction of funds required to resolve the issue
  • Disciplinary action arising from an SLA breach
  • Jurisdiction disputes between departments
  • Final acceptance that a case is genuinely closed

09 · Governance

Data, accountability and the limits of the system

A proposal of this kind fails on governance long before it fails on technology. This section states the boundaries explicitly so they can be examined before approval rather than discovered afterwards.

Where data lives

  • Data remains within the department's existing systems and the state's own infrastructure. The mission reads and writes; it does not become the system of record.
  • Access follows the department's current role-based rules. A block officer sees their block; a field worker sees their households.
  • No beneficiary data is shared outside government. Aggregate views are available to the administration for planning only.
  • Every read, write and escalation is logged with a timestamp and an identity, so any case can be reconstructed after the fact.
  • The district can export everything and switch the system off at any point without losing departmental data.

What the system will not do

CategoryPosition
Clinical decisionsNo diagnosis, no treatment recommendation, no triage that substitutes for a Medical Officer.
Benefit denialThe system never removes, reduces or withholds an entitlement. It can only flag a case for human review.
Disciplinary actionSLA breaches are reported as process facts. Any action against an individual is a departmental decision.
Financial sanctionNo fund release, no procurement decision, no payment authorisation.
Legal determinationNo adjudication of land, eligibility, jurisdiction or dispute.
Autonomous enforcementNothing is executed in the field without a named human acting on it.
The principle in one line

The system prepares the decision and records what happened. An authorised officer takes the decision and remains accountable for it.

Handling failure

The proposal assumes the system will sometimes be wrong. What matters is that being wrong is detectable and correctable.

  • Every flag is advisory and can be dismissed by the responsible officer with a recorded reason.
  • Dismissal reasons are reviewed during the pilot to calibrate thresholds rather than being ignored.
  • Where underlying data is missing or stale, the system states its uncertainty rather than producing a confident wrong answer.
  • A weekly review with the department is part of the pilot, not an optional extra.

Integration posture
